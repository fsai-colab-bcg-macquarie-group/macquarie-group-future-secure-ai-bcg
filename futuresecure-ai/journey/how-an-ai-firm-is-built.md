# How an AI firm is built

An educational case study of **Future Secure AI Inc.**, assembled from public sources: the company website, press releases, job advertisements, and news reporting. It is a teaching note on how a modern AI delivery company can be assembled — not an official history, and not a claim about private internal facts.

Last compiled: 10 September 2026. Every named fact below has a source. Where reporters disagree or data is third-party, that is labelled.

---

## 1. The pattern, in one page

Future Secure AI is a useful public example of a **lighthouse-client company**:

1. A founder who already sits next to a large enterprise buyer (here: Macquarie Capital).
2. A second founder who can sell the idea to boards (here: a former McKinsey partner and growth author).
3. A first customer who is also an investor — “the classic case of the first investor also being the first client,” as people familiar with the Macquarie partnership told *The Australian Financial Review*.
4. A **new category name** (AI Co-Workers / Red Zone) so the firm is not competing as generic SaaS, systems integration, or hourly consulting.
5. A leadership bench hired from the same industries and firms as the buyers (banks, PE, healthcare, BCG, Deloitte, McKinsey).
6. A geographic move toward the capital and buyers (Sydney → Austin HQ, 2026).

That sequence — **buyer intimacy → category language → borrowed credibility → scaled delivery bench** — is the educational core of this page.

## 3. How it was created (from public fragments)

### 3.1 Two complementary founders

Public leadership copy and press name:

- **Mehrdad Baghai** — Co-Founder & Co-CEO. Former McKinsey partner; described on the site as a serial entrepreneur and three-time best-selling author on growth and collective action.
- **Michael Hunter** — Co-Founder & Co-CEO. Former investment banker at **Macquarie Capital**, investing in technology companies from early- to late-stage.

The pairing is the classic **strategy-credibility + buyer-access** founding team. One founder can talk to CEOs in McKinsey language; the other already knows how a global investment bank buys and funds technology.

### 3.2 First customer = first capital

September 2025 *AFR* reporting: Macquarie both **backs** the start-up and **trials** the product in finance and HR. People familiar with the deal called it the textbook first-investor-is-first-client pattern. Macquarie did not disclose economics.

**Teaching point:** an AI “consulting” company that wants product multiples usually starts as a **captive-looking joint build** with one regulated buyer, then generalises the playbook. Job ads still say the firm was “created in deep partnership with one of the world’s largest financial institutions.”

### 3.3 What they actually sell (the product, not the pitch deck)

From the 2026 website:

- **AI Co-Workers** — personas wrapped on multi-agent systems; they exist on the organisation chart and execute end-to-end workflows.
- **Red Zone** — high-complexity, mission-critical work. Explicitly positioned *above* “Blue Zone” plug-and-play agents (10–15% productivity in press copy) and *below* “Black Zone” private-data / ML shops.
- **Build *and* run** — not a one-off implementation. Press: on-network, LLM- and cloud-agnostic, multi-year (five-to-ten-year) operating lifecycle, “Mission Control.”
- Commercial claim in HQ press: payback “within six months,” then “10x to 20x” operating cost. These are **company claims**, not independently audited figures on this page.

Named example Co-Workers on the site: Tate (NAV analyst), Astra (client concierge), Amy (month-end analyst), Jack (recruiter).

### 3.4 What they insist they are *not*

The live site (Sep 2026) is explicit: “a true partnership, not SaaS, not black boxes, not systems integration or consulting.”

**Teaching point:** category design is a go-to-market act. If you look like a consultancy, you get consulting fees. If you look like horizontal SaaS, you get compared to every chatbot. “AI Co-Worker / Red Zone” is an attempt to price like an **operated digital employee** rather than a licence or a timesheet.

## 4. Mission language — what changed

Public wording moved from **10x slogan + avatars** (2025 hiring / AFR) to **category + governance** (2026 website / HQ press).

| Theme | Earlier public language (2025) | Later public language (2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission | “Humans and AI agents will work together and give everyone the ability to get 10x performance.” | “Bridge the gap between LLMs and rapid ROI.” “Global leader in Red Zone AI Co-Workers.” |
| Metaphor | Digital workers / avatar employees with names and faces (*AFR*). | Digital colleagues on the org chart; hybrid human + AI teams. |
| Buyer | “Jointly developed with one of the world’s largest financial institutions.” | Global 2000, PE portfolios, “large enterprises and private equity.” |
| Proof | Stealth; little public detail (*AFR*). | Named quotes (Macquarie, Healius, Orrick, FIS, TOGA); three anonymised case studies with dollar/impact claims. |
| Geography | Sydney / Australia hiring. | Austin HQ; eight hubs (Austin, New York, Chicago, Toronto, Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Auckland). |
| Values | Job ads: **BRAVER** (courage, rigor, curiosity). | Same BRAVER copy still on Greenhouse in 2026. |
| Delivery model | Agile startup with C-suite relationships. | Partnership that designs, deploys, **and runs**; Mission Control; 100+ agents. |

This is a normal company-building arc: **origin myth stays** (bank partnership, 10x humans+AI), while **external language professionalises** so US enterprise buyers and PE sponsors can put it in a board pack.

## 7. Educational playbook — how a firm like this can be created

These are lessons **drawn from the public story**, for students of AI company-building. They are not advice from Future Secure AI.

### Lesson A — Start inside a buyer, then incorporate

Hunter’s Macquarie Capital background plus a Macquarie trial/investment is the origin. Many durable AI delivery firms begin as an **internal build or JV** that is later wrapped in a Pty Ltd / Inc.

### Lesson B — Pair a domain founder with a narrative founder

Banker + McKinsey author is a go-to-market machine. Engineering can be hired (Hillin from Cohere). Story and access are harder to hire on day one.

### Lesson C — Name a category, refuse the old ones

“Not SaaS, not SI, not consulting” is a pricing strategy. Students should notice the **Red / Blue / Black Zone** map: it tells the buyer where to put the PO.

### Lesson D — Staff the firm like the client’s executive committee

The public bench is Macquarie + McKinsey + BCG + Deloitte + a few platform specialists. That is how you staff **trust**, then delivery. A pure research lab will not get month-end close in a bank.

### Lesson E — Put advisors where the next cheque lives

Moore (Macquarie), Hunt (Australian public sector/health), Burwell (US health policy), PE-facing BCG alumni. Advisors are distribution, not decoration.

### Lesson F — Move HQ when the buyer geography moves

Sydney origin, Austin HQ once the pitch is Global 2000 / US PE. Press is explicit: “the center of gravity for enterprise AI is the United States.”

### Lesson G — Keep the first client’s work unnamed in case studies, named in quotes

Case studies stay generic (“global FS firm”). Testimonials name people. That is a standard way to show proof without over-disclosing.

### Lesson H — Run, don’t only build

The “Mission Control” and five-to-ten-year run story is how a services-looking firm argues it has **recurring revenue** (an operated Co-Worker, not a project).

### Lesson I — Be honest about what is not public

Funding amounts, ownership, Vietnam/Brazil org design, and real utilisation of 282 people are **not** in the sources above. Third-party databases contradict each other (some even confuse this company with an unrelated “Future Secure” in Tel Aviv). An educational reader should **discard unsourced valuation widgets**.
